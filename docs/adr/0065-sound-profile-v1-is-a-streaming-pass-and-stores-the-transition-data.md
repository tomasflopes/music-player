# 0065: Sound profile v1 is a streaming pass and stores everything a beat-synced transition needs

Date: 2026-11-15 · Status: accepted · Supersedes the shape of 0055 (ordered float list); amends the line format of 0056

**Context.** Owner decision 2026-11-15: the profile must already hold what a later beat-synced, DJ-style transition needs, so the 16k-song Mac run is not repeated when that feature is built (a new feature set means a new `profiler_version` and a full re-run). Beat grid, intro/outro and the energy curve need the whole song, and a whole-song `FloatArray` does not fit the phone's default heap (ADR 0054, memory finding). The result is no longer a fixed list of floats.

**Decision.**
- **Streaming pass.** `SoundProfiler` has `version` and `newStream(sampleRate): ProfileStream`; a stream takes mono float chunks of any size (`feed`) and returns a `SoundProfile` at `finish()`. Inside, a streaming windowed-sinc resampler brings the audio to 22.05 kHz (ADR 0054), then one Hann-windowed FFT 1024/512 per frame feeds running accumulators. Only per-frame scalars (RMS, onset strength, ~43 per second, about 40 KB for a 4 min song) are kept, never the PCM. The three-window rule of §7.5 is dropped for v1: all 8 features come from the whole song, one pass, one code path. Results are bit-identical for the same samples whatever the chunking (outputs are computed from absolute sample positions, in double precision).
- **Hand-written FFT**, no library (unchanged from 0055); it also serves chroma and the spectrum features.
- **Stored in this order.** (1) The 8 v1 features, unchanged in meaning and position: tempo (folded to 60–180 BPM), mean loudness (mean frame RMS), loudness variation (standard deviation of frame RMS), spectral centroid (Hz, mean over frames weighted by frame RMS), bass energy ratio (20–250 Hz share of the energy), onset density (onsets per second), beat strength (normalised autocorrelation of the onset envelope at the beat period, 0–1), zero-crossing rate (per sample, mean over frames weighted by frame RMS; a crossing only counts between samples beyond ±0.001, because MediaCodec returns 16-bit PCM that rounds the faint pre-echo between clicks to zero while ffmpeg's float keeps it; measured on the emulator). (2) Beat grid: first-beat time in seconds and tempo in BPM, rounded to 0.1 BPM. (3) Tempo confidence, 0–1. (4) Three tempo candidates (BPM and relative score, best first; unused slots 0). (5) 12 raw chroma bins, C first, summed energy over the whole song scaled to sum 1 (zeros for silence); the lowest bins are coarse at this FFT size, so key is derived later and is unreliable on bare beats. (6) Intro/outro boundaries in seconds: music start, intro end, outro start, music end. (7) Energy curve: RMS of each whole second (one float per second, the last second may be partial).
- **Tempo.** The onset envelope is spectral flux of log-compressed magnitudes, high-passed with a 1 s moving mean. Tempo is the peak of a Fourier-tempogram scan from 50 to 220 BPM in 0.1 BPM steps (score = magnitude at f plus half the magnitude at 2f), then folded into 60–180. This replaces the "autocorrelation" wording of §7.5; same family, fine resolution without interpolation. Candidates are the three best distinct peaks (a peak is distinct when it is the maximum within ±2 % in BPM), which is where half and double tempo show up. Tempo confidence is the best peak's share of the three candidate scores, rescaled so an even split reads 0 and a lone peak reads 1. Beat strength and tempo confidence are multiplied by a pulse factor from 0 to 1 (mean of the high-passed envelope over the mean of the raw envelope, ramped from 0.15 to 0.35, times a level ramp from 0.05 to 0.5 on the mean raw envelope, so a mathematically pure tone counts for nothing): a click track reads about 0.9, a held tone 0.1–0.2, noise 0.03, so a drone or noise does not claim a confident beat. First beat: the phase of the onset envelope at the folded tempo, read at the end of the frame an onset enters, taken as the first beat at or after the music start (within 50 ms).
- **Intro/outro.** From per-frame RMS: music start is the first run of 5 frames above max(0.001, 1 % of the median frame RMS); intro end is the first time the 1 s moving mean of frame RMS reaches 70 % of its median; outro start is the last time it is at least 70 % of that median; music end is the last non-silent frame. Always music start ≤ intro end ≤ outro start ≤ music end.
- **BLOB (Room, ADR 0058).** Raw float32 little-endian, no header: a fixed part of 33 floats in the order above (8 + 2 + 1 + 6 + 12 + 4), then the energy curve. The curve length is implied by the size, `size / 4 − 33`. `profiler_version` stays a column of its own, so a layout change is a version change.
- **Export (JSON Lines, 0056).** The header line keeps `profiler_version`, the fixed feature names (33, in order) and the music root hint. Each song line holds `path` (NFC, relative), `size`, `durationMs`, `fixed` (the 33 floats, shortest float32 text) and `energy` (the curve). Written with the kotlinx-serialization JSON element API, no compiler plugin. Expected size: 2–3 KB a line, 40–50 MB for 16k songs; accepted for one file that is copied once (no compression, so it stays readable and diffable).
- **Mac CLI.** `analyser analyse --root … --out profiles.jsonl [--threads N]`: parallel, one progress line, resumable (done songs are skipped by path and size; a half-written last line is ignored and cut off; a file with another `profiler_version` stops the run). Songs that fail to decode are listed and not written, so a rerun retries them.

**Consequences.** One pass costs a full decode: about 0.2 s on the Mac, 14 s median on the phone (ADR 0054), so the phone only analyses songs the Mac import did not cover. Whole-song features make the seek-decode of ADR 0054 unusable for the profiler; it stays a benchmark rule. Tolerances in ADR 0060 are judged on the 8 v1 features and the tempo; the new fields get golden values but no cross-decoder tolerance until the first golden run says what is realistic. Changing any definition above changes `profiler_version` and forces a new Mac run. Open for the next phase, not this one: beat-synced transitions themselves, key from chroma, time-stretching.

**Measured (2026-11-15, Mac, 554-song sample, 10 cores).** 554 songs in 68 s of wall time, 0 failed; 0.94 s of CPU per song, so about 33 min for 16k songs. The export is 1.6 MB for the sample, about 45 MB for 16k songs. On real music tempo always lands in 60–180, the four boundaries are always ordered, the energy curve has one value per second, and the first beat is never before the music starts. Tempo confidence is **not calibrated**: a share of three peak scores gives a median of 0.11 and 59 songs at 0.3 or more, so a mix that refuses to sync below some confidence would refuse about half of this library. There is no ground truth for tempo here; the candidate scores are stored (best, second and third, relative to the best), so a better confidence rule can be derived later without a new run, but the pulse factor is not stored. The MediaCodec profile of the generated test files agrees with the ffmpeg golden on the emulator within the tolerances of `GoldenCompare`; the first run found the zero-crossing difference described above, and the dead band fixed it.
