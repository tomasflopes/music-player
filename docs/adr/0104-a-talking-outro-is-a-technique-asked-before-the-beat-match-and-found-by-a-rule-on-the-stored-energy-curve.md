# 0104: A talking outro is a technique asked before the beat match, found by a rule on the stored energy curve

Date: 2026-11-23 · Status: proposed · Extends 0092, 0065

**Context.** Phase 7 slice 4: when the outgoing song ends with someone talking, the next song's beat should enter under the talk. Profile v1 has no speech detector and no new `profiler_version` is allowed (ADR 0065), so the guess has to come from stored data. CLAUDE.md asks for a new implementation behind the technique seam, not a branch in the beat match.

**Decision.** `TalkOutroTechnique` (priority 10, the existing `TALKING_OUTRO_PRIORITY`, so before the beat match at 20: the owner chose this on 2026-11-23 over priority 30) is a new `TransitionTechnique`, one `@IntoSet` line. `TalkDetector` is pure: the tail (outro start to music end, the music end capped at the song's duration) is talk when it lasts at least `talkMinMs` (8 s) and its mean is at most `talkEnergyRatio` (0.5) of the mean of the body (intro end to outro start). The means are of the stored per-second RMS (amplitude, so 0.5 is -6 dB, not half the power); a second's bin belongs to a range when the middle of that second lies in it. A missing or short curve, a value that is not a level, an empty body or a silent body is "not talk". The plan is a plain `TransitionPlan.Overlap` (speed 1, no ease-back, no bass, no grids, so the engine runs it unchanged): the incoming song starts at outro start + `talkLeadMs` (1 s) on its first bar and the overlap lasts to the music end, capped at `maxOverlapMs` (20 s) and at what the incoming song has left, under 4 s it is null. `TalkCurve` is the gain: the incoming song rises over 1 beat; the outgoing one ducks by `talkDuckDb` (-6) over 1 bar (linear in dB), holds, and falls on a quarter cosine over the last `talkFadeBars` (4) bars of the overlap, each part shortened to fit. When the incoming tempo is not trusted (`TempoReliability`, ADR 0099) the song starts at its music start, rises over a fixed second and the bar is a fixed 2 s; an incoming song with no readable profile gives null. Anything null falls to the beat match, then the crossfade.

**Consequences.** A talk outro is taken even when the tempos would beat-match, since the tail has no beat to match. The rule cannot tell talk from a quiet, flat musical tail, and a long fade-out of a loud song that averages under half the body also passes it: `scripts/talk_check.py` reports how many flagged tails decay, and a shape guard is the named next step. Tested on synthetic curves, virtual players and a fake clock; no run on real songs or on the phone yet.
